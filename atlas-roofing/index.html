What we heard
A few things that came up
in our conversation.
  • A new hire's first day consists of mind-numbing training videos and safety PowerPoints. This leaves a first impression that, whatever is to come, is going to be a slog.
  • Almost everything is taught by tribal knowledge. Passed across twenty or thirty people, the message waters down until a new hire is learning something that was never even true.
  • You already know the first day sets the tone — people "ought to feel welcomed, ought to feel like you really want them here." Right now nothing carries that but a PowerPoint.
  • And the turnaround is real: a plant that once churned close to 100% now loses almost no one, and people walk in with the community, not away from it. That story deserves to be told on day one.

The approach
Documentary — not a training video.

The moment a new hire hears "training video," they brace for stilted performances and eighties stock music. We go the other way: real people, the real floor, shot with care and cut for energy. It's the difference between something people endure and something they remember — and because it's built on what's genuinely true here, it earns their trust instead of testing their patience.

Shot well and grounded in what's real, these films don't go stale. This isn't a reel you re-do every time something changes — it's an asset the Ardmore plant welcomes people with for years.
